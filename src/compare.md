## Coverage at a glance

<p class="section-intro">“Available” means the metric can answer the stated question—not that every source has identical granularity.</p>

<div class="surface-card table-scroll compare-coverage-table">
  <table class="briefing-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Lens</th><th>Strongest common measure</th><th>What remains limited</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td><strong>Scale</strong></td><td>Reported contracts by day</td><td>Open interest and intraday liquidity</td></tr>
      <tr><td><strong>Products</strong></td><td>Sports/non-sports and broad categories</td><td>Venue taxonomies require mapping</td></tr>
      <tr><td><strong>Parlays</strong></td><td>Share of volume where identified</td><td>Product flags and outcome detail</td></tr>
      <tr><td><strong>Trading behavior</strong></td><td>Trade-size distributions where prints exist</td><td>Aggressor and participant identity</td></tr>
      <tr><td><strong>Economics</strong></td><td>Published fee schedules</td><td>Realized revenue and bettor P&amp;L</td></tr>
      <tr><td><strong>Accuracy</strong></td><td>Actual versus implied outcomes</td><td>Sample size, settlement history, and void handling</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

<div class="chart-note"><strong>Comparison rule:</strong> when a metric is only credible for one venue, it stays in that venue’s deep dive rather than becoming a one-row “industry” ranking.</div>
